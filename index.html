Industrial IoT signal conditioning, data acquisition and data reduction

With your algorithms running at the sensor.

Roseate Technology LLC designs hardware and firmware that condition sensor signals, reduce them to meaningful measurements, and publish the results to SCADA, PLCs and real-time controllers, inside the controller’s cycle.

Capabilities

Signal conditioning

Analog front ends for industrial sensors: excitation, amplification, filtering and precision conversion for clean, high-bandwidth measurements. Reads IEEE 1451.4 TEDS sensor IDs to set up channels automatically and report each sensor’s identity to your systems.

Data reduction

On-device post-processing that takes signals sampled in the kilohertz range and reduces and combines them into the values that matter, down to a single pass or fail health signal. Built-in RMS, spectra and envelope analysis, plus reductions your own engineers define and run in the node. Or stream signals to your supervisory system at up to full bandwidth, limited only by the transport, including UDP push to any listener on the network.

Presentation

A built-in web interface for commissioning and diagnostics, with live views of the signal at every stage of processing.

Publishing to supervisory systems

The node can act as a real-time device in your controller’s EtherCAT® or PROFINET® network, delivering results every cycle. Modbus® TCP and OPC UA® serve SCADA, PLC and plant historian systems, and MQTT with Sparkplug B serves IIoT platforms. All data is time-synchronized and can be timestamped from IEEE 1588 PTP or a plant or lab reference clock, so measurements line up across every node and system.

Packaging

DIN-rail modules for the control cabinet first, then sealed, IP-rated housings for mounting at the machine, and bespoke mechanical packaging designed in-house to your requirements.

About

Roseate Technology is a new, independent engineering company in Minnesota, developing its first products. We focus on practical, well-documented hardware for engineers who need dependable measurements from the equipment they monitor, delivered to the systems they already use.

Lillian

Our firmware is named Lillian, after Lillian “Lil” Rosenow, the founder’s mother. She was a switchboard operator who spent her working life connecting each call to the person it was meant for. Lillian does the same job for signals: it takes what the sensors say and connects it to the systems and people who need it.
